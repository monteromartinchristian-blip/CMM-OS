## 1. Purpose

Phase 10.50 introduces **Domain Privacy Policies** as declarative, domain-owned privacy defaults that specialize the existing canonical Phase 8 privacy system without creating a second privacy engine, resolver, permission model, provider policy, redaction system, or cross-domain authority mechanism.

The objective from the Phase 10 roadmap is:

> Define default privacy behavior for each Domain Pack while preserving resource-level, workflow-level, and operation-level overrides.

The effective privacy decision must remain **monotonic and most-restrictive** across every applicable privacy source. A Domain may contribute a default, but it may never grant itself authority or widen a stricter upstream or downstream constraint.

Phase 10.50 is therefore an **adapter/declaration phase**, not a new execution subsystem.

## 3. Source requirements

The Phase 10.50 roadmap requires:

1. per-Domain default privacy behavior;
2. preservation of resource-, workflow-, and operation-level constraints;
3. effective policy composition across:
   - global privacy policy;
   - user policy;
   - session policy;
   - resource policy;
   - Knowledge Package policy;
   - Domain policy;
   - workflow policy;
   - operation policy;
   - model-provider policy;
4. the most restrictive applicable policy to prevail unless an authorized exception exists;
5. Domains must not:
   - grant themselves remote access;
   - weaken `LOCAL_ONLY`;
   - export restricted packages;
   - send sensitive data to prohibited providers;
   - preserve sensitive outputs in unauthorized caches;
   - transfer information to supporting domains without permission;
   - omit privacy decisions from Domain Trace.

The roadmap's historical illustrative `DomainPrivacyPolicy` contains fields that overlap with later canonical Phase 8 `PrivacyMetadata` and Phase 10.15 permissions. This specification reconciles that historical sketch with the **actual repository contracts** rather than duplicating them.

`cmm/cognitive/privacy.py` is the canonical processing-policy owner.

The repository already contains:

- `PrivacyPolicy`;
- `ProcessingLocation`;
- `PrivacyOperation`;
- `PrivacyDecisionStatus`;
- immutable `PrivacyMetadata`;
- `PrivacyResolution`;

- `evaluate_privacy_operation(...)`;
- `privacy_from_resource(...)`;
- `privacy_from_knowledge_item(...)`;
- `privacy_from_knowledge_package(...)`.

The implemented canonical `PrivacyPolicy` hierarchy is:

- `LOCAL_ONLY`;
- `LOCAL_PREFERRED`;
- `REMOTE_ALLOWED`;
- `PREMIUM_ALLOWED`.

`SENSITIVE` is **not** a processing-policy value in the canonical implementation. Sensitivity is represented separately by `cmm.cognitive.enums.SensitivityLevel`.

This separation is mandatory:

```text
SensitivityLevel
    = nature / criticality of the content

ResourcePermission / ResourcePermissionOperation
    = who may do what

PrivacyMetadata / PrivacyPolicy
    = where/how content may be processed, cached, exported,
      redacted, approved, or transmitted
```

Phase 10.50 must not merge these concepts.

### 4.2 Phase 10.15 permissions

The following existing infrastructure remains authoritative:

- `cmm/domains/permission_contracts.py`;

- existing external-model, external-search, resource, workflow, operation, export, approval and cross-domain permission checks.

Phase 10.50 must not add a second `allow_cross_domain` authority.

A Domain privacy default can make processing **more restrictive**, but cross-domain access is still authorized only through the Phase 10.15 permission chain.

Phase 10.50 must consume the resulting canonical package privacy through `privacy_from_knowledge_package(...)` and must not reinterpret the Phase 10.49 floor as remote-processing permission, export permission, provider permission, cache permission, or approval authority.

## 5. Architectural decision

### 5.1 Chosen design

Introduce a small immutable `DomainPrivacyPolicy` contract that wraps the canonical `PrivacyMetadata` processing policy and adds only Domain-specific declaration semantics that do not already belong to Phase 8 or Phase 10.15.

Conceptually:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class DomainPrivacyPolicy:
    schema_version: str
    domain_id: DomainId
    default_privacy: PrivacyMetadata
    require_approval_for_remote: bool = False
    metadata: Mapping[str, JsonValue] = field(default_factory=dict)
```

The exact repository JSON-value alias and metadata freeze helper must reuse existing Domain conventions.

The contract must be:

- immutable;
- typed;
- versioned;
- deterministic;
- serializable;
- strict on unknown fields;
- free of credentials/secrets;
- provider-independent;
- model-independent;
- non-executable;
- incapable of granting authority by itself.

### 5.2 Why `default_privacy: PrivacyMetadata`

The historical roadmap sketch repeats fields already owned by `PrivacyMetadata`, including:

- allowed processing locations;
- allowed providers;
- prohibited providers;
- remote processing;
- premium processing;
- cache;
- export;
- redaction;
- approval.

Duplicating those fields would create two privacy schemas and eventually two sources of truth.

Therefore `DomainPrivacyPolicy` owns Domain identity and Domain-specific declarative intent, while the actual processing-policy payload is the canonical `PrivacyMetadata`.

### 5.3 Why `allow_cross_domain` is excluded

`allow_cross_domain` is deliberately **not** part of the new contract.

Cross-domain authority is already owned by Phase 10.15 through `DomainPermissionPolicy` and the canonical permission resolver/gate. Reintroducing it in privacy policy would create competing authority and ambiguous composition.

Privacy may restrict a transfer after permission is granted. Privacy may never grant the transfer.

## 6. Roadmap reconciliation: `SENSITIVE`

The Phase 10.50 roadmap uses the historical shorthand:

```text
Health        -> SENSITIVE
Relationships -> SENSITIVE
...
```

The current repository has since made the concepts explicit and separate. Therefore, for Phase 10.50:

> `SENSITIVE` in the roadmap is an **orientation label**, not a new `PrivacyPolicy` enum member.

A Domain with a sensitive orientation must express that through a canonical combination such as:

- `PrivacyPolicy.LOCAL_ONLY` or another approved processing policy; and
- canonical `SensitivityLevel.SENSITIVE` or stronger, as appropriate.

Phase 10.50 must **not** add `PrivacyPolicy.SENSITIVE`.

This reconciliation is normative for the implementation.

## 7. DomainDefinition integration

`DomainDefinition` receives one additive optional field at the end of its compatibility surface:

```python
privacy_policy: DomainPrivacyPolicy | None = None
```

Requirements:

1. Existing definitions with no privacy policy continue to deserialize and behave compatibly.
2. Serialization is deterministic.
3. Declarative pack parsing must support the field through the existing `DomainDefinition` / `ParsedDomainPack` path.
4. Unknown or malformed privacy-policy payloads fail closed with a Domain privacy contract/serialization error.
5. No alternate privacy block is introduced in `DomainManifest` if `DomainDefinition` can carry the contract through the existing declarative path.
6. The implementation must follow the same additive compatibility discipline used by recent DomainDefinition extensions.

`General` is intentionally allowed to keep `privacy_policy=None`, representing the roadmap requirement that General privacy is resolved per operation/context rather than through one Domain-wide grant.

## 8. Projection into canonical PrivacyMetadata

Phase 10.50 may provide a **thin pure projection**, but not a new privacy resolver.

The allowed responsibility is equivalent to:

```text
DomainPrivacyPolicy
    ↓ pure projection
PrivacyMetadata
    ↓
resolve_effective_privacy_metadata(...)
    ↓
evaluate_privacy_operation(...)
```

The projection may:

- return `default_privacy` unchanged for ordinary operations;
- add a canonical `requires_approval=True` requirement when
  `require_approval_for_remote=True` and the attempted operation is remote;
- preserve all canonical `PrivacyMetadata` fields exactly;
- add provenance/reference information using canonical metadata/inheritance fields if appropriate.

The projection must never:

- change `LOCAL_ONLY` to a broader policy;
- turn `allow_remote=False` into `True`;
- turn `allow_export=False` into `True`;
- turn `allow_cache=False` into `True`;
- remove prohibited providers;
- widen allowed-provider intersections;
- lower sensitivity;
- remove redaction;
- remove approval;
- override resource/package privacy;
- execute approval itself;
- execute redaction itself;
- call a provider;
- perform export;
- perform caching.

The canonical Phase 8 resolver/evaluator remains the sole privacy decision authority.

## 9. `require_approval_for_remote` semantics

`require_approval_for_remote` is an **additional obligation**, never an authorization.

The invariant is:

```text
approval requirement ≠ permission to process remotely
```

Examples:

- Domain default is `LOCAL_ONLY`, `require_approval_for_remote=True`:
  - remote processing remains denied;
  - an approval cannot widen `LOCAL_ONLY`.

- Domain default is `REMOTE_ALLOWED`, `allow_remote=True`,
  `require_approval_for_remote=True`:
  - remote processing may proceed only if every other applicable privacy and permission constraint allows it;
  - canonical privacy evaluation additionally requires approval.

- Resource/package policy is `LOCAL_ONLY` while Domain is `REMOTE_ALLOWED`:
  - effective policy is still `LOCAL_ONLY`.

No Phase 11 exception/widening mechanism is implemented here.

## 10. Effective privacy composition

Phase 10.50 must never create `DomainPrivacyResolver`, `DomainPrivacyEngine`, or equivalent competing owner.

All effective processing-policy composition goes through:

```python
resolve_effective_privacy_metadata(...)
```

Inputs may include, as available in the existing call path:

```text
global privacy
user privacy
session privacy
resource privacy
Knowledge Package privacy
Domain privacy
workflow privacy
operation privacy
provider/model privacy
```

The canonical rules remain authoritative, including:

- most restrictive `PrivacyPolicy`;
- highest sensitivity;
- intersection of allowed processing locations;
- intersection of allowed providers where applicable;
- union of prohibited providers;
- restrictive boolean composition for remote, premium, cache and export;
- additive redaction requirements;
- additive approval requirements.

An absent layer does not create authority. A Domain default is only one input.

### 11.2 Interpretation rules

1. The table is a Domain-level default, not final effective authority.
2. Resource-derived privacy may always make the result stricter.
3. Knowledge Package privacy may always make the result stricter.
4. Phase 10.15 permissions may deny an operation even when privacy allows it.
5. Provider/model privacy may make the result stricter.
6. No Domain-level provider allowlist is a provider-selection mechanism.
7. `allowed_providers=None` in canonical semantics means the Domain adds no provider-specific allowlist restriction; it must not be interpreted as provider authorization.
8. A concrete provider remains subject to all later provider/model/privacy/permission gates.
9. `allow_export=False` is the default for all first-party policies in 10.50 unless an already-authoritative lower-level contract is stricter; Phase 10.50 does not introduce export grants.
10. `allow_premium=False` is the Domain default; premium/model routing remains separate.

    package_privacy,

## 13. Permission and cross-domain interaction

The authorization equation is:

```text
privacy allows
AND
permissions allow
AND
required approvals are satisfied
AND
other canonical runtime gates allow
```

Not:

```text
privacy allows
=> operation authorized
```

For cross-domain transfer:

1. source Domain permission must allow transfer;
2. target Domain permission must allow reception/access where applicable;
3. canonical cross-domain constraints must pass;
4. privacy must allow the transfer/processing;
5. effective sensitivity and provider/location restrictions must pass;
6. required approval/redaction must be satisfied.

A failure at any layer blocks the operation.

## 14. Provider/model boundary

Phase 10.50 is provider-independent and model-independent.

It must not:

- add concrete provider IDs to first-party Domain code as routing choices;
- create a provider registry;
- create a model catalog;
- create a model gateway;
- choose models;
- implement provider fallback;
- implement Phase 11 Model Gateway policy;
- execute remote calls.

Canonical `allowed_providers` / `prohibited_providers` fields may be preserved when supplied by an already-authoritative privacy input, but Domain privacy defaults must not become a routing subsystem.

- an export engine;

- anonymization;

## 16. Domain Trace requirement

The roadmap requires privacy decisions not to be omitted from Domain Trace.

Phase 10.50 must reuse the existing Phase 10.17 Domain Trace/evidence/reference mechanism.

Requirements:

- no new trace subsystem;
- no duplicated payload persistence;
- no provider secrets or sensitive raw payloads added to trace merely for privacy evidence;
- trace stores/reference-links the canonical privacy decision or its existing safe reference/evidence representation;
- privacy decision identity/status/reason codes must remain auditable;
- if the existing trace contract already provides a generic evidence/reference seam, use it;
- if a new additive typed reference field is genuinely required, it must extend the canonical trace contract rather than introducing a privacy-specific trace store.

The implementation plan must identify the exact existing trace seam before coding this integration.

## 21. Design Point

### `DP-050 — Domain Privacy Defaults`

A Domain Pack may declaratively contribute an immutable, typed, versioned and serializable `DomainPrivacyPolicy` that projects into the canonical Phase 8 `PrivacyMetadata` contract.

The Domain policy is a **restrictive input only**. It participates in the canonical most-restrictive privacy resolution and canonical privacy-operation evaluation without:

- granting remote authority;
- widening `LOCAL_ONLY`;
- weakening resource or Knowledge Package policy;
- replacing sensitivity;
- replacing permissions;
- replacing provider/model policy;
- replacing approvals;
- creating a second privacy resolver/engine/store/registry/runtime.

`DP-050` additionally requires all currently implemented first-party Domain Packs to expose their approved Domain privacy default or, for General, explicitly expose no Domain-wide default so privacy remains operation/context resolved.

## 22. Connected Acceptance Test

### `AT-DP-050`

The acceptance test must exercise **real canonical components**, not isolated mocks.

Minimum connected path:

```text
real first-party DomainDefinition
    ↓
real DomainRegistry / canonical Domain Pack path
    ↓
DomainDefinition.privacy_policy
    ↓
DomainPrivacyPolicy → canonical PrivacyMetadata projection
    ↓
real canonical KnowledgePackageBuilder
    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

```

### Required acceptance scenarios

#### A — `LOCAL_ONLY` cannot be widened

- Domain default permits remote or is less restrictive.
- Resource or package policy is `LOCAL_ONLY`.
- Effective policy is `LOCAL_ONLY`.
- Remote operation is denied.
- Approval must not convert denial into permission.

#### B — sensitive Domain remains local by default

Use a real sensitive first-party Domain such as Health.

Prove:

- real DomainDefinition carries its Domain privacy policy;
- canonical package privacy is derived;
- effective privacy remains local-only;
- remote processing is denied;
- export is denied;
- prohibited provider remains blocked if introduced by another privacy input.

#### C — remote-allowed Domain is only conditionally remote

Use a real University, Oppositions or Languages Domain.

Prove:

- Domain default may be `REMOTE_ALLOWED`;
- remote is possible only when all other privacy inputs and permissions permit it;
- a stricter resource/package input forces `LOCAL_ONLY`;
- Domain default does not create provider authorization.

#### D — remote approval is additive

Use a policy with `require_approval_for_remote=True`.

Prove:

- allowed remote operation without approval yields canonical approval-required status;
- same operation with canonical approval satisfied may continue only if no other gate denies;
- local-only input remains denied even with approval.

#### E — cache/export restrictions survive composition

Compose a permissive input with a stricter Domain/resource/package input.

Prove:

- `allow_cache=False` survives;
- `allow_export=False` survives;
- redaction requirement survives;
- approval requirement survives.

#### F — provider restrictions survive composition

Prove canonical provider semantics:

- allowed-provider constraints intersect;
- prohibited providers accumulate;
- a prohibited provider cannot be made valid by a Domain default.

#### G — General does not synthesize a Domain grant

Prove:

- real General Domain definition has no Domain-wide privacy grant;
- effective privacy is resolved from operation/resource/package/context inputs;
- missing Domain policy does not become `REMOTE_ALLOWED`.

#### H — cross-domain permission remains Phase 10.15 authority

Prove:

- privacy alone cannot authorize a cross-domain transfer;
- a permission denial blocks even if privacy would otherwise allow processing;
- an allowed permission path can still be blocked by privacy.

#### I — 12 first-party definitions

Verify the exact current inventory:

```text
general
health
relationships
university
oppositions
reflection
concerns
languages
parenthood
sport
life_plan
project
```

Every first-party definition must have the approved Domain privacy posture; General is the explicit no-default case.

#### J — declarative round trip

Prove the canonical pack path accepts and deterministically round-trips the new privacy policy without a second parser.

#### K — trace evidence

Prove the canonical privacy decision or safe decision reference is observable through the existing Domain Trace evidence/reference seam without persisting secret/raw restricted payload content.

## 29. Final architectural statement

Phase 10.50 does **not** create a new privacy subsystem.

It gives Domain Packs one safe declarative way to say:

> “For this Domain, start at least this restrictive.”

The actual answer to:

> “May this specific information, in this specific operation, at this specific time, be processed here, remotely, cached, exported, or sent to this provider?”

continues to belong to the canonical Phase 8 privacy resolver/evaluator, combined with the canonical permission, approval, workflow, provider and runtime gates.

That boundary is the central invariant of `DP-050`.
